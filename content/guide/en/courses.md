# Courses, textbooks, and MOOCs

A course gives you a structured way to learn a subject; a textbook lets you work carefully through definitions and derivations; an official tutorial connects ideas to working code. Start with something you want to understand or do, then choose one main resource and one supplement. Use this selection to find what you need, without feeling you have to complete everything.

Browse **69 entries: 5 MOOC platforms and 64 courses, textbooks, or official tutorials**. The Chinese and English pages list the same resources. Each entry describes the language, recommended background, topics, and a suggested way to put what you learn into practice.


For self-study, distinguish access to materials from enrollment, assessment, certificates, and academic credit. Check the teaching language, edition, prerequisites, and feedback options. Read one sample section and attempt an exercise before committing to a sequence: the amount of support it gives matters more than the institution’s name.

## Choose the task first

| Task | Prioritize | Evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write a data-processing program independently | CS50P or the Python tutorial, plus an engineering exercise | Tests, error handling, repeatable execution |
| Understand training, generalization, and evaluation | One ML course, plus probability and linear algebra | A leakage-free baseline and error analysis |
| Enter a research area | One specialist course, then original papers on a focused question | An explanation of the task, assumptions, competing approaches, and open questions |
| Improve training or deployment efficiency | A systems course and a profiling experiment | Speed, memory, and quality under matched conditions |

Use the [learning path](learning-path.md) to plan your studies, [foundations](basics.md) to review prerequisites, or [your first AI project](start.md) to apply the ideas. An introductory survey gives you an overview; a full course takes you through concepts and exercises in sequence; a research seminar assumes a grounding in the subject and explores selected questions. Choose the format that meets your needs. Where two offerings share modules, work through the common material once and use the second for a specific missing explanation.

## Time series and forecasting

Study models and inference with Murphy's textbooks, then use Bayesian Modeling and Computation in Python to work through priors, data, posterior inference, and predictive checks. Use pgmpy for small graphical models. Check assumptions, prior sensitivity, inference diagnostics, and posterior predictions rather than presenting a credible interval alone.

- **[Probabilistic Machine Learning · Kevin P. Murphy](https://probml.github.io/pml-book/)** — **Type: author-maintained textbook series and code; language: English; recommended background: linear algebra, calculus, probability, and ML.** Presents supervised learning, latent variables, graphical models, and approximate inference from a probabilistic perspective. Choose the introductory or advanced volume to suit your task. **Suggested output:** state a small model's generative assumptions, likelihood, and inference procedure, comparing analytic, sampling, or approximate solutions.
- **[Bayesian Modeling and Computation in Python](https://bayesiancomputationbook.com/welcome.html)** — **Type: author-maintained online textbook and code; language: English; recommended background: Python, probability, statistics, and regression.** Covers inference, model exploration, linear and extended models, time series, and complete workflows. **Suggested output:** a report with prior checks, inference diagnostics, and posterior predictive checks. Consult the authors' updated notebooks and environment files when running code.

## Audio and speech

Study sampling, spectra, and evaluation with Aalto's book, then choose a speech or dialogue task from CS224S and try a small SpeechBrain experiment. Check sample rates, speaker splits, text normalization, WER/CER, and noise-related errors. Read-speech results do not directly establish conversational performance.

- **[Aalto · Introduction to Speech Processing](https://speechprocessingbook.aalto.fi/)** — **Type: university online textbook; language: English; recommended background: basic linear algebra, calculus, probability, and signals.** Covers representations, preprocessing, analysis, recognition, synthesis, enhancement, and evaluation. **Suggested output:** explain waveform and time-frequency views of the same audio, then change noise or preprocessing and measure its effect on a defined task.
- **[Stanford CS224S · Spoken Language Processing](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224s/)** — **Type: university course and assignment materials; language: English; recommended background: Python and basic ML/NLP.** Connects audio analysis, speech recognition and synthesis, dialogue management, and interactive systems. **Suggested output:** a small recognition or dialogue experiment reporting speaker/language differences, latency, and failures. Lecture recordings in the current archive require enrollment.

## Search, multi-agent learning, and numerical optimization

Choose CS188 for the foundations of planning and decision making; study MARL after single-agent RL, distinguishing multiple learning actors from repeated calls to one language model. Pair Algorithms for Optimization with EE364a for numerical methods. Check search or update rules, evaluation budgets, constraints, and convergence diagnostics.

- **[UC Berkeley CS188 · Introduction to Artificial Intelligence](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/archive/sp25/)** — **Type: archived university open course; language: English; recommended background: Python, data structures, and basic probability.** Covers search, constraint satisfaction, game trees, MDPs, RL, and probabilistic inference, with projects. **Suggested output:** compare two search or game strategies, documenting optimality conditions, expanded nodes, and failure cases.
- **[Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Foundations and Modern Approaches](https://marl-book.com/)** — **Type: author-maintained textbook, Python code, and exercises; language: English, with a Chinese print edition listed by the authors; recommended background: probability, optimization, programming, and single-agent RL.** Covers games, solution concepts, multi-agent learning, and environments. **Suggested output:** compare policies in a small cooperative or competitive environment, recording training/execution information, opponent distributions, and multiple seeds. Account for nonstationarity when attributing changes.
- **[Algorithms for Optimization · Kochenderfer & Wheeler](https://algorithmsbook.com/optimization/)** — **Type: author-maintained textbook and code; language: English, primarily Julia code; recommended background: linear algebra, calculus, probability, and programming.** Introduces local search, first-/second-order methods, stochastic search, constraints, and surrogates through engineering problems. **Suggested output:** compare gradient-based and direct search under equal function-evaluation budgets, checking feasibility and stopping rules. Start with the first edition; the second edition remains labeled a preview.
