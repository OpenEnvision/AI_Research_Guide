# Courses, textbooks, and foundations

Choose one main course or textbook, then use shorter explanations to resolve a specific gap. Crash Course gives an overview, 3Blue1Brown builds mathematical intuition, and structured courses provide exercises that test understanding. An animation can make a result intuitive; checking its assumptions and working through an example help you understand it precisely.

## Computing: what computers and programs do

- **[Crash Course Computer Science](https://thecrashcourse.com/topic/computerscience/)** — Short English videos hosted by Carrie Anne Philbin. Start with binary, CPUs, programs, operating systems, files, and the internet to build an overall picture. [Official introduction](https://thecrashcourse.com/courses/crash-course-computer-science-preview/)

- **[CS Self-Learning Guide](https://csdiy.wiki/)** — A Chinese course directory with learners' accounts of self-study. Read the introduction and usage guide, then find a course for the subject you need.

- **[The Missing Semester of Your CS Education](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/)** — Lectures and exercises in English on everyday computing tools. Start with the shell, Git, and debugging to learn command-line tools, version control, and troubleshooting.

- **[Pro Git, English edition](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2)** — An online book. Begin with basic operations, history, and remote collaboration; study branches when working with others. A [Chinese edition](https://git-scm.com/book/zh/v2) is also available.

## Mathematics: intuition, foundations, and depth

- **[3Blue1Brown: Linear Algebra](https://www.3blue1brown.com/?topic=linear-algebra)** — Visual explanations in English of vectors, transformations, matrix multiplication, and eigenvectors. The geometry helps explain why the formulas look as they do. [Bilibili channel](https://space.bilibili.com/88461692/)

- **[3Blue1Brown: The Essence of Calculus](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/essence-of-calculus/)** — English videos and illustrated text. Build intuition for rates of change, small changes, and integration, then connect these ideas to gradients and the chain rule.

- **[Seeing Theory — Brown University](https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/)** — Interactive probability and statistics. Change parameters and inspect distributions and samples to understand the concepts.

- **[MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra — Gilbert Strang](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/)** — English lectures, notes, and assignments from 2010. Study subspaces, orthogonality, eigenvalues, and SVD for a structured grounding in linear algebra.

- **[Harvard Stat 110 — Joe Blitzstein](https://stat110.hsites.harvard.edu/)** — English lectures, textbook, and exercises. Conditional probability, expectation, and common distributions recur in ML and RL; pair concepts with exercises.

- **[Mathematics for Machine Learning](https://mml-book.github.io/)** — An open English textbook connecting mathematics to ML. Consult matrix decompositions, vector calculus, probability, and optimization.

## Machine learning and deep learning

- **[An Introduction to Statistical Learning](https://www.statlearning.com/)** — An English-language textbook and accompanying courses, with Python and R editions. Start with training, generalization, regression, and classification, then model selection and resampling; choose algorithms by task later.

- **[Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai/)** — An English textbook with code. Work through data operations, linear models, losses, optimization, and multilayer perceptrons, then explore the architectures that interest you. Also available: the [Chinese textbook](https://zh.d2l.ai/) and [Chinese course index](https://courses.d2l.ai/zh-v2/).

- **[Hung-yi Lee's 2025 Machine Learning Course](https://speech.ee.ntu.edu.tw/~hylee/ml/2025-spring.php)** — Chinese explanations, slides, and videos using generative AI to explain ML concepts. Select the lectures relevant to your question.

- **[Andrew Ng: Machine Learning Specialization](https://www.deeplearning.ai/specializations/machine-learning)** — A course sequence connecting Python exercises with supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and common algorithms.

- **[Stanford CS229](https://cs229.stanford.edu/)** — A systematic English course on algorithm assumptions, derivations, and statistical learning. You can also turn to the notes for a specific question.

- **[Practical Deep Learning for Coders — fast.ai](https://course.fast.ai/)** — A practical course in English for people with some programming background. Build a working model first, then understand the components; begin with lesson one and its exercises.

- **[3Blue1Brown: But what is a Neural Network?](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/neural-networks/)** — Visual videos and text. Build a picture of inputs, layers, parameters, and outputs, then see how gradient descent and backpropagation fit together.

- **[StatQuest with Josh Starmer](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCtYLUTtgS3k1Fg4y5tAhLbw)** — Short English videos offering another explanation of cross-validation, bias and variance, tree models, and other statistics or ML concepts.

## Python, tensors, and your first training loop

- **[CS50's Introduction to Programming with Python](https://cs50.harvard.edu/python/)** — A beginner course in English with exercises. Move from functions, conditions, and loops to exceptions, libraries, and files; practice explaining and changing small programs.

- **[NumPy: the absolute basics for beginners](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/absolute_beginners.html)** — The official introduction to array computing, in English. Focus on shapes, indexing, axes, and broadcasting.

- **[PyTorch: Learn the Basics](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/basics/intro.html)** — An official tutorial in English that brings data, models, autograd, optimization, and model saving together in a complete training workflow. Useful for reading your first training loop.

- **[Neural Networks: Zero to Hero — Karpathy](https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html)** — English videos and code requiring Python and basic mathematics. Begin with micrograd to see how gradients are computed, then move into language models.

## Architectures: diagrams, code, and derivations

- **[The Illustrated Transformer — Jay Alammar](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/)** — English diagrams connecting token representations, attention, encoders, and decoders. Trace inputs and outputs before returning to equations.

- **[The Annotated Transformer — Harvard NLP](https://github.com/harvardnlp/annotated-transformer)** — Annotated English code and notebooks pairing the original Transformer paper with implementation. A useful next step after the diagrams if you want to understand the tensors and modules in code.

- **[Transformer Explainer — Polo Club](https://poloclub.github.io/transformer-explainer/)** — Interactive visualization of inputs moving through a Transformer and how components affect next-token prediction.

- **[Hung-yi Lee: Self-Attention and Transformers](https://speech.ee.ntu.edu.tw/~hylee/ml/2021-spring.php)** — Chinese lectures from the 2021 machine-learning course explaining self-attention and Transformers.

- **[Understanding LSTM Networks — colah](https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-08-Understanding-LSTMs/)** — An illustrated English article from 2015. Learn LSTMs through states, gates, and sequence processing; useful alongside time-series models.

- **[Scientific Spaces: article archive — Jianlin Su](https://kexue.fm/content.html)** — Chinese technical archive. Follow the Transformer and diffusion series for positional encodings, architectures, and mathematical derivations.
