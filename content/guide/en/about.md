# About this guide

The OpenEnvision AI Learning & Research Guide is an open reference for **university students and junior researchers worldwide**, including learners outside computer science and those studying independently. It brings together foundational ideas, hands-on projects, and research methods, with an emphasis on work you can explain, evaluate, and reproduce.

The guide is maintained by [OpenEnvision](https://openenvision.org/). This edition builds on [Takamatsu-Hikaru/AI-Research-Guide](https://github.com/Takamatsu-Hikaru/AI-Research-Guide) and credits the original authors wherever their writing and experiences are included or adapted.

## Who it is for

- **Beginners:** build a foundation in programming, mathematics, and machine learning through exercises and projects you can run locally.
- **Students starting research:** explore a field, learn to read papers, find guidance, and take a first project from question to results.
- **Junior researchers:** improve experimental design, evaluation, engineering, writing, and collaboration.

You may be an undergraduate or graduate student, joining your first research project, or studying alongside a job. Start wherever your current knowledge and goals lead you. A project may require specific skills, equipment, data access, or supervision; the guide helps you work out what you need for the next step. Use it alongside your curriculum, project requirements, and advice from people familiar with your circumstances.

## How the guide is organized

The [learning path](learning-path.md) connects [foundations](basics.md), [courses](courses.md), and [practice](practice.md). The [research map](directions.md) and [specialized topics](advanced-topics.md) help you turn a broad interest into a specific question or task. Chapters on [reading](reading.md), [experimental design](research-methods.md), [data and evaluation](datasets.md), [engineering](engineering.md), [responsible AI](responsible-ai.md), and [writing](writing.md) support a complete research project.

The English and Chinese editions share learning paths, resources, and learning objectives. External resources remain in their original languages, so an English guide page may link to material in another language. Adapted community essays credit their authors and distinguish personal experience from general guidance.

## Editorial standards

We give priority to resources from the original authors, teaching institutions, official documentation, and original research publications. Course entries identify resource type, language, prerequisites, and suggested outcomes. The research chapters distinguish suggested exercises from experiments that have actually been run, results from interpretations, and specific findings from broader claims.

Examples and schedules are teaching suggestions. Recommendations should be adapted to the question and available resources. Check the requirements of the institution, program, or publication you are working with. The research-group directory is a selection of examples, not a ranking or a complete survey of global institutions.

The content was revised on **2026-10-17**. Individual resource pages explain what was checked. Courses, software versions, licenses, and access policies change. We have not rechecked every older link or assignment; the revision date does not mean every external resource was verified that day.
