# Lookout: what are researchers and labs working on?

Curious about what researchers and labs are working on beyond your courses? Start with a demo, article, or interview that interests you, then follow it to the paper and code. The sources below can help you find work to explore next.

## Meet people through their work

When a demo makes you curious, find the original project. Read about the problem and method, then explore the authors' other work and the groups behind related papers. Over time, you will start to recognize the people and ideas in a research community.

For example, [ACT / ALOHA](https://tonyzhaozh.github.io/aloha/) can lead you from demonstrations and action modeling to questions about robot datasets and policy learning. [ReAct](https://react-lm.github.io/) can lead you from tool use and feedback to longer tasks, error recovery, and evaluation.

Write down questions worth asking, then [contact an author, mentor, or fellow researcher](contact.md).

## Places to look

The company directory above collects original research and product announcements. To explore research topics, use **[Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/)** to search a topic and follow authors and related work.

**[Berkeley AI Research Blog](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/archive/)**  
See how lab members introduce and develop a research question.

**[Hugging Face Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers)**  
Discover papers and discussions, then follow the links to the original papers, code, and author pages.

Conference sites provide another route to papers, talks, tutorials, and workshops. Start with [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/), [ICML](https://icml.cc/), or [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/), then browse the year and track relevant to your question. Check the publication and review record of the specific work.

## Media and personal blogs

Chinese-language technology outlets include [Jiqizhixin](https://www.jiqizhixin.com/), [QbitAI](https://www.qbitai.com/), and [Xinzhiyuan](https://aiera.com.cn/). They cover papers, open projects, products, and industry developments. Follow a news report to its original paper or release before relying on a technical claim.

[Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/), [Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/), and [Xiaohongshu](https://www.xiaohongshu.com/) carry Chinese-language explanations, courses, interviews, and personal accounts. [X](https://x.com/) and [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/) also host researcher updates and technical discussions in multiple languages. Choose material by its author, evidence, and relevance to your question.

**[Z Potentials](https://zpotentials.substack.com/)**  
Interviews with AI teams, founders, and technical practitioners. See what different teams are working on, why they chose a problem, and how their technology becomes a product.

**[Funeral AI](https://funeralai.substack.com/)**  
Chinese writing on AI products, tools, and industry developments. Explore how new products work and what people are debating.

Personal blogs show process as well as outcomes. [Jianlin Su's Chinese articles](https://kexue.fm/content.html) explore mathematics and model questions, [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/) records tool experiments, and [Karpathy](https://karpathy.ai/) shares courses, projects, and essays. See [technical blogs and research perspectives](blogs.md).

## Prepare a research brief

[The research brief workflow](irene-workflow.md#brief) explains how to select new papers, code, and technical articles around a research question, remove duplicates, and set an update schedule. Review a first brief before deciding how often to prepare one.

For each item you keep, note the original link and date, what problem it addresses, what differs from related work, and one question to pursue. A short, question-driven list is more useful than an exhaustive feed. Use your [reading purpose](reading.md#reading-depth) to decide which items need deeper study.

## Research communities and knowledge sharing

### OpenEnvision

[OpenEnvision (OE)](https://openenvision.github.io/) is an open AI research community connecting academia and industry, with interests in world models, multimodal intelligence, vision, and embodied AI. It also shares research knowledge through curated writing, interviews, and courses.

- **[BlogrXiv: AI research blogs and technical writing](https://openenvision.github.io/BlogrXiv/site/index.html)** brings together research blogs, lab essays, and technical notes. Browse by field for explanations, engineering experience, and research methods, then follow links to the original articles.
- **[ScholarTube: AI interviews, podcasts, and courses](https://openenvision.github.io/ScholarTube/)** collects long-form researcher interviews, video podcasts, complete courses, and research talks across agents, world models, vision, robotics, and research practice, with links to the original videos.

You can also recommend articles and videos to [BlogrXiv](https://github.com/OpenEnvision/BlogrXiv) and [ScholarTube](https://github.com/OpenEnvision/ScholarTube).

### Lumina

[Lumina](https://lumina-embodied.ai/) brings together embodied AI research, open projects and community events. Its [Embodied AI Guide](https://github.com/TianxingChen/Embodied-AI-Guide) organizes the field’s learning resources; talks, research coverage, and events on the website introduce the people and projects behind the work.

### AgentHub

[AgentHub](https://hqhq1025.github.io/agent_hub/) collects discussions from the agent community into daily and weekly digests, covering agent research, tools, engineering practice and industry developments. Browse by date, discussion group or external news to see what people are working through, how they compare technical approaches and where their views differ.
