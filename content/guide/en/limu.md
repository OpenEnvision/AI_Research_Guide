# Mu Li: deep learning courses and paper walkthroughs

Mu Li's teaching materials can help at two stages. When learning to train models, use Dive into Deep Learning to connect concepts with code. When you begin reading research, his paper walkthroughs show how to examine abstracts, figures, methods, and experiments.

## Starting out: study and modify a training run

Use the [English Dive into Deep Learning textbook](https://d2l.ai/). The [Chinese textbook](https://zh.d2l.ai/) and Mu Li's [Chinese course and video index](https://courses.d2l.ai/zh-v2/) offer corresponding material; the [course opening](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1if4y147hS/) is on Bilibili. Start with data operations, linear models, losses, and optimization, then multilayer perceptrons. Read the code as you go: what shape is the input, how is the loss calculated, and how are the parameters updated?

If you need a project, try the [classification route](start.md). For mathematics, return to [foundations](basics.md). Other explanations include [fast.ai, 3Blue1Brown, Hung-yi Lee, and CS229](catalog-foundation.md#topic-5).

## First papers: read independently, then compare

The [paper-reading index](https://github.com/mli/paper-reading) collects Mu Li's Chinese video discussions. Begin with [How to Read a Paper](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1H44y1t75x/), then choose a paper you are studying:

- [Transformer](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1pu411o7BE): attention and the encoder–decoder architecture.
- [ViT](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV15P4y137jb/): applying Transformers to image patches.
- [CLIP](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1SL4y1s7LQ/): image–text contrastive learning.

First, write down your understanding of the question, method, and experiments. After watching, note where he paused, which figures you overlooked, and which claims need experimental support. Use those observations to develop your own approach to reading papers.
