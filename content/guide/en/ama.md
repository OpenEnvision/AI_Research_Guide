# Questions and contributions

This guide grows through contributions from learners and researchers with different backgrounds. Wherever you study or work, and whatever stage you are at, you can ask about the material, correct an error, or suggest a useful public resource.

## Choose a starting point

For common questions about preparation, topics, experiments, and publication, consult the [60-question research handbook](research.md). For help finding a mentor or starting a collaboration, see [academic contact and collaboration](contact.md). To choose what to study next, use the [learning path](learning-path.md).

If an explanation in the guide is unclear or incomplete, raise it in this project's issue tracker. The tracker is for questions about the guide and suggestions for improving it. For help debugging a particular project, applying to a program, or seeking a collaboration, use the contact channels provided by that project or team.

## Make your question easy to understand

Focus each issue on one question and include:

- **Context and goal:** what you want to understand or accomplish.
- **Relevant location:** a page link, passage, or resource name.
- **Previous attempts:** your current interpretation, what you have tried, and what you observed.
- **Requested help:** what remains unclear or what explanation would help.

Use a small, publicly shareable example when including code or data. You do not need to demonstrate that you have mastered every prerequisite; explaining your current understanding makes useful feedback easier. Replies depend on contributors' availability, so individual guidance cannot be guaranteed.

## Report an error or suggest a resource

If you find an error in an explanation or translation, a broken link, or a page problem, tell us where it is and suggest a correction. Include a primary source when a factual or research claim is involved. When suggesting a course, paper, dataset, or tool, explain its intended audience, the problem it helps with, its language and prerequisites, and any known access restrictions.

Contributions in either language are welcome. A change in one language is useful too; identify the corresponding page so maintainers can check the other version. See the [contribution guide](https://github.com/OpenEnvision/ai_research_guide/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) for file locations and editing instructions.

## Keep the conversation useful

Search existing issues before posting, and add relevant information to an existing thread where appropriate. After receiving help, record what worked and what remains unresolved. Focus on the evidence and ideas, and respect differences in educational background, language, and access to resources.
