# AI Learning & Research Guide

An open guide for **university students and junior researchers worldwide**. Build a solid foundation in AI, carry out reproducible projects, and learn how to turn a question into a study others can evaluate. Start with what you already understand and can do, whatever your degree, year of study, or institution.

The guide connects programming and mathematics with machine learning, research areas, literature review, study design, research engineering, and academic communication. The English and Chinese editions cover the same core topics and resources. Look up a chapter when you have a question, or follow a learning path at your own pace. You do not need to read the whole guide in order.

## Learn through a complete research cycle

1. **Understand:** follow one main course and fill in the background you need for your task.
2. **Implement:** inspect the data, establish a simple baseline, and record the environment and configuration.
3. **Investigate:** formulate a question, design a fair comparison, and examine both results and failure cases.
4. **Communicate:** connect each claim to evidence, explain limitations, and share materials that others can inspect.

Use the [course directory](courses.md) to find a structured course and the [practice platforms](practice.md) to work on specific skills. Keep a short note of what you understand, what evidence you checked, and what remains unresolved. A research contribution may be theoretical, empirical, methodological, or a carefully justified analysis of data or evaluation; it need not introduce a larger model.

## Perspectives and community

The guide is maintained by [OpenEnvision](https://openenvision.org/). Related community projects include [BlogrXiv](https://openenvision.github.io/BlogrXiv/site/index.html), a collection of AI technical writing, and [ScholarTube](https://openenvision.github.io/ScholarTube/), a directory of researcher interviews, courses, and talks.
